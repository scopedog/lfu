Two-node LFU: stock lfs find vs the offloaded Object Stream (LU-20721)
=====================================================================
2026-09-10.  Server 192.168.122.10 (MGS + lustre-MDT0000 + 2 OSTs, loop
files), client 192.168.122.101, both rhel9.7 VMs on one host over virbr1.
Fixture: the 2026-09-09 llmount filesystem, 88,230 MDT inodes.
Predicate: -mtime -1 -type f.  Caches dropped on BOTH nodes before each run.
Stock lfs find is ~/lustre-release 50ecccfb61, no LFU in it.
Offload arm is tests/lab-rpc/offfind.c over LL_IOC_LFU_SCAN, one MDT.

Counts, and why they differ:
  87944  stock lfs find      namespace-visible, a walk
  87946  lfind --target      + 2 nameless MDT objects a walk cannot reach
  87958  lfind --internal    + 12 internal objects (no LMA / NOT_IN_OI)
  87958  offload harness     identical to lfind --internal: the harness's
                             predicate is validated against the real one

Caveats:
 - the harness is a spike consumer, not lfs find.  It spells the predicate
   out; its answer is checked against lfind's real predicate code, which is
   what the 87958 = 87958 line above is for.  A finished lfs find would
   apply the classifier and land on 87946.
 - one MDT.  Several would parallelise the offload and not the walk.
 - client and server are VMs on one host: virbr1, not a NIC.  A real
   network widens this, since only the stock arm pays per-round-trip.
 - stock's user+sys (10.9 s) exceeds its wall (8.8 s): it burns more than
   one core on RPC processing.  The offload spends 0.01 s of CPU.
